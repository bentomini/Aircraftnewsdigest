## Directly Fleet-Relevant

**A321neo AD 2026-14-05 mandates RMP/DRAIMS software fix for comms-loss risk, effective Aug 14** — A321neo, 2026-08-14.
*What happened:* FAA AD 2026-14-05 (Amendment 39-23406), effective August 14, 2026, supersedes AD 2025-11-02 and requires a permanent Airbus DRAIMS software modification on A321neo variants — including the -251NX/-252NX/-253NX/-271NX/-272NX subtypes CX operates — addressing reported loss of radio-management-panel (RMP) data synchronization that can cause total loss of communication means, including transponder and standby navigation functions.
*Technical detail:* AD 2026-14-05 (Amendment 39-23406), A321-251N, -251NX, -252N, -252NX, -253N, -253NX, -253NY, -271N, -271NX, -271NY, -272N, -272NX; FAA: unsafe condition is addressed by a permanent DRAIMS software fix (upgrade to L4.3 standard, including RMP and audio-management software); AD also prohibits installation of affected parts. Reported loss of synchronization between radio management panels (RMPs) affecting DRAIMS, which can cause total loss of VHF communications, transponder and standby navigation functions. MCAI basis: EASA AD 2025-0118R1, dated July 15, 2025. [VERIFIED — primary source] ([source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-10/html/2026-13983.htm))
*Verbatim:* "loss of communication means (RMP data synchronization and very high frequency communications) on the digital radio and audio integrating management system (DRAIMS)" — Airworthiness Directives; Airbus SAS Airplanes (AD 2026-14-05), 2026-14-05 / Amendment 39-23406, 2026-07-10 (effective 2026-08-14), [link](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-10/html/2026-13983.htm).

**Air India A320neo (AI2379) sequential hydraulic-system failure triggers 300ft altitude drop; DGCA/Airbus probe continues** — A320neo family, 2026-08-04. *(carried-over developing event)*
*What happened:* Air India flight AI2379 (A320neo, Phuket-Delhi) suffered an approximate 300-foot altitude drop on August 4, 2026, injuring up to 24 of 145 aboard. Preliminary trade-press reporting attributes it to sequential loss of hydraulic pressure across the Green, Blue and Yellow systems, an autopilot disconnect, and elevator fault messages. India's DGCA seized flight recorders and brought Airbus into the investigation; reports of hydraulic-pressure loss as the working theory continued through August 14, 2026, with an AAIB (India) probe ongoing. A separate captain toxicology finding has not been causally linked to the event by any official source. ([reporting](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323222/20260805/air-india-a320neo-plunge-blamed-possible-fly-wire-fault-not-turbulence.htm))
*Technical detail:* Reported as a suspected sequential Green/Blue/Yellow hydraulic pressure loss; root cause not confirmed by any publicly issued investigator finding as of this run. [REPORTED — trade press, unconfirmed]
*Read-across to Cathay Pacific fleet:* A320neo family shares core flight-control and hydraulic system architecture with CX's A321neo; if traced to a common-mode design or maintenance issue, this event would carry direct precedent for A321neo hydraulic-system oversight.
