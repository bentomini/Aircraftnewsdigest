**FAA AD 2026-10-06 supersedes 2020-24-08: Trent 700 HPT blade corrosion-fatigue replacement, effective June 12, 2026** — A330-300, 2026-06-12.
*What happened:* The FAA superseded AD 2020-24-08 with new AD 2026-10-06 for Rolls-Royce Deutschland RB211 Trent 768-60, 772-60, and 772B-60 engines (Trent 700 series, A330 powerplant), effective June 12, 2026. The AD responds to high levels of corrosion fatigue in HPT blades that could result in blade cracking and release, with risk of aborted takeoff and in-flight shutdown events; it requires removal of certain engines from service and HPT blade replacement before specified flight-cycle thresholds, with an optional engine modification as terminating action.
*Technical detail:* AD 2026-10-06 (Amendment 39-23346; Docket No. FAA-2026-4635; Project Identifier MCAI-2025-00213-E), Rolls-Royce Deutschland Ltd & Co KG (RRD) Model RB211 Trent 768-60, 772-60, and 772B-60 engines as identified in EASA AD 2018-0291R1, dated February 24, 2025 (Trent 772C-60 excluded — no FAA type certificate); FAA: unsafe condition addressed by removal from service of certain engines and replacement of HPT blades before exceeding specified flight cycles since new, with an optional engine modification eliminating the need for repetitive replacement; this AD incorporates EASA AD 2018-0291R1 by reference in its entirety, except stated exceptions. Trent 772C-60 excluded as it has no FAA type certificate; High-pressure turbine blades on certain Trent 700 (RB211 Trent 768-60/772-60/772B-60) engines subject to high levels of corrosion fatigue, dependent on environmental, operational, and individual blade conditions; risk of blade failure with increased risk of high-energy debris release, damage to the airplane and reduced control of the airplane. **FAA AD 2026-12-05: A350 primary flight control actuators exposed to overload during acceptance testing; effective July 20, 2026** — A350-900, A350-1000, 2026-06-15.
*What happened:* The FAA published final AD 2026-12-05 for all Airbus A350-941 and -1041 airplanes, confirming that certain primary flight control actuators (servo controls, electrical backup hydraulic actuators, electro-hydrostatic actuators) were exposed to mechanical overloads during the manufacturing acceptance test procedure, reducing fatigue life. The AD requires replacement of affected actuators with serviceable parts and prohibits installation of affected parts.
*Technical detail:* AD 2026-12-05 (Amendment 39-23375; Docket No. FAA-2026-3473; Project Identifier MCAI-2025-01221-T), All Airbus SAS Model A350-941 and -1041 airplanes, certificated in any category; FAA/EASA: replacement of affected parts with serviceable parts required; installation of affected parts prohibited. EASA AD 2025-0152, dated July 18, 2025, is the underlying MCAI, incorporated by reference. FAA estimates this AD affects 38 airplanes of U.S. registry, at $850 cost per product ($32,300 total on U.S. operators); During production, certain primary flight control actuators were exposed to mechanical overloads during the acceptance test procedure, leading to reduced fatigue life; uncorrected, this could result in actuator failure, loss of control of control surfaces, or hydraulic system loss, and reduced control of the airplane. [VERIFIED — primary source] ([source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-15/pdf/2026-11975.pdf))
*Verbatim:* "certain primary flight control actuators have been exposed to mechanical overloads during the acceptance test procedure, leading to reduced fatigue life of the affected actuators" — Airworthiness Directives; Airbus SAS Airplanes (AD 2026-12-05), AD 2026-12-05, Federal Register Vol. 91, No. 114, June 15, 2026, [link](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-15/pdf/2026-11975.pdf).

**FAA AD 2026-09-14: A350 oxygen generator retaining clamp obsolescence/incorrect torque spec; effective June 18, 2026** — A350-900, A350-1000, 2026-06-18.
*What happened:* FAA AD 2026-09-14, effective June 18, 2026, applies to all Airbus A350-941 and -1041 airplanes. It addresses obsolescence of the clamp holding the chemical oxygen generator in its container: Collins introduced a replacement clamp from another manufacturer with a different locking-torque specification that was not properly reflected in Airbus documentation, risking damage to the oxygen generator housing if installed at the wrong torque. The AD requires replacing the affected clamp, prohibits use of an outdated maintenance task revision, and prohibits installation of affected parts.
*Technical detail:* AD 2026-09-14 (Amendment 39-23336; Docket No. FAA-2026-1323; Project Identifier MCAI-2025-01190-T), All Airbus SAS Model A350-941 and -1041 airplanes, certificated in any category; FAA: installing a part using the incorrect (not-updated) torque value could lead to damage of the chemical oxygen generator housing, reducing available oxygen capacity when needed, possibly resulting in injury to occupants. EASA AD 2025-0138, dated July 1, 2025, is incorporated by reference. FAA estimates this AD affects 38 airplanes of U.S. registry, up to $260,050 cost per product (up to $9,881,900 total on U.S. operators); Obsolescence of original oxygen-generator retaining clamp; Collins-sourced replacement clamp from a different manufacturer has a different locking-torque specification not properly reflected in Airbus documentation, risking generator housing damage and reduced oxygen capacity if installed at the incorrect torque. [VERIFIED — primary source] ([source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-05-14/pdf/2026-09659.pdf))
