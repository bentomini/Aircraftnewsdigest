## Directly Fleet-Relevant

**AAIA preliminary report: Cathay Pacific A330-343 B-HLQ tail contacted runway during go-around at HKG; structural cracking found in aft fuselage** — A330-300, 2026-07-31.
*What happened:* Hong Kong's Air Accident Investigation Authority (AAIA) released Preliminary Report PLR-2026-02 (dated 31 July 2026) on the 3 July 2026 accident involving Cathay Pacific A330-343 B-HLQ (MSN 0420), arriving from Narita. During a go-around initiated for operational reasons on approach to Runway 07R, the rear of the aircraft contacted the runway; the aircraft landed safely with no injuries. Post-flight inspection found lower-fuselage abrasion (FR69-FR85), damaged potable-water/waste service doors, and — beyond initial trade-press reports — deformation/cracking in aft cargo-compartment floor beams and a 10-inch crack in the aft pressure bulkhead.
*Technical detail:* PLR-2026-02 [VERIFIED — primary source] ([source](https://www.tlb.gov.hk/aaia/doc/PLR-2026-02_ENG.pdf)), B-HLQ (MSN 0420) only - single-aircraft accident investigation, no fleet-wide effectivity; AAIA: "no analysis or findings are included in this report"; investigation team is "conducting a detailed analysis of the data and information collected in order to determine the circumstances and causes of this accident."
*Verbatim:* "During the go-around procedure the rear of the aircraft came into contact with the runway surface." — Accident Investigation Preliminary Report and Public Notice PLR-2026-02 - Airbus A330-343, B-HLQ, Hong Kong International Airport, 3 July 2026, PLR-2026-02, 2026-07-31, [link](https://www.tlb.gov.hk/aaia/doc/PLR-2026-02_ENG.pdf).

**EASA AD 2025-0274R1 (Fire Panel Inspection) covers CX's A321neo; BALPA renews warning after continued fire-switch shutdown reports** — A321neo, 2026-08-03.
*What happened:* BALPA (UK pilots' union) issued a renewed warning on 3 August 2026 about uncommanded in-flight engine shutdowns caused by self-releasing A320-family fire pushbuttons. The underlying EASA directive is AD 2025-0274 (issued 9 Dec 2025, effective 16 Dec 2025, superseding AD 2025-0253 then AD 2025-0234; current revision R1 issued 7 July 2026, effective 14 July 2026), mandating fire-panel inspection/replacement across A318/A319/A320/A321 including CX's A321neo NX variants. Note: the scanner's originally-claimed reference, AD 2026-0134, was confirmed to be an unrelated Elevator/Aileron Computer directive and has been corrected here.
*Technical detail:* AD 2025-0274 rev. R1 [UNVERIFIED — reference exists, text not accessed] ([source](https://ad.easa.europa.eu/blob/EASA_AD_2025_0274.pdf/AD_2025-0274_1)), Airbus A318/A319/A320/A321 family, all manufacturer serial numbers, including A321-251NX, A321-252NX, A321-253NX, A321-271NX, A321-272NX (CX A321neo variants) - confirmed from base AD 2025-0274 (9 Dec 2025) applicability list; current R1 revision (7 Jul 2026) applicability confirmed unchanged via ad.easa.europa.eu canonical page; EASA: uncorrected condition "could result in an in-flight engine(s) shut down and consequent reduced aeroplane controllability." SAFRAN (fire-panel/switch manufacturer) and Airbus issued SB/VSB/SIL guidance; AD mandates inspection/replacement of affected fire-panel P/Ns, with expedited 6-month replacement for parts on a listed serial-number appendix and 24-month inspection for others; Per fetched AD 2025-0274 text: "Occurrences were received of one engine fire switch self-releasing, inducing an uncommanded in-flight shut down of one engine." TAIC's Air New Zealand A320neo investigation (Dec 2024 event) additionally attributes this to a fire-panel retaining pin bent ~2.73 deg (vs +/-1 deg tolerance) during supplier (SAFRAN) mishandling - that specific figure is sourced from taic.org.nz (tier-3 investigator), not independently confirmed against the EASA AD text itself.

**EASA PAD 26-087 proposes inspection of A350-900/-1000 fire integrated control panel after in-flight engine shutdown** *(carried-over developing event)* — A350-900, A350-1000, 2026-07-21.
*What happened:* EASA issued Proposed AD 26-087 (21 July 2026) covering Airbus A350-941/A350-1041 (A350-900/A350-1000) aeroplanes fitted with fire integrated control panel (ICP) FIN 245VM, P/N 727-7206-03, after an in-service occurrence in which an engine fire switch self-released in flight and caused an uncommanded engine shutdown. The PAD proposes a one-time inspection per Airbus ISB A350-31-P067; public consultation closes 18 August 2026.
*Technical detail:* PAD 26-087 [PROPOSED — not yet final] ([source](https://ad.easa.europa.eu/blob/EASA_PAD_26_087.pdf/PAD_26-087_1)), Airbus A350-941 and A350-1041 aeroplanes, all manufacturer serial numbers (fire ICP FIN 245VM, P/N 727-7206-03); EASA: condition, if uncorrected, "could result in an in-flight engine(s) shut down and consequent reduced control of the aeroplane"; proposes one-time GVI of the affected ICP within 24 months of the AD's effective date, replacement if damage found. Airbus has revised production so aeroplanes with reference date after 01 August 2026 receive parts not subjected to mechanical shock pre-installation; Per fetched PAD text: a mechanical shock to the fire integrated control panel (FIN 245VM) caused internal damage to the fire switch locking mechanism, allowing the switch to self-release in flight and command an engine shutdown.
*Verbatim:* "one engine fire switch self-released in flight, causing an engine in-flight shut-down (IFSD)" — Notification of a Proposal to issue an Airworthiness Directive - PAD 26-087, ATA 31 Integrated Control Panels - Inspection, 26-087, 2026-07-21, [link](https://ad.easa.europa.eu/blob/EASA_PAD_26_087.pdf/PAD_26-087_1).
