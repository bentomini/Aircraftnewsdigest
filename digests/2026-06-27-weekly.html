NTSB UPS MD-11 (Flight 2976, docket DCA26MA024) investigative hearing — May 19-20, 2026: aft-pylon bearing fatigue findings VERIFIED [carried-over developing event]
· 2026-05-19

What happened: NTSB held a two-day investigative hearing (19-20 May 2026, docket DCA26MA024, confirmed on ntsb.gov) into the 4 November 2025 crash of UPS Flight 2976, an MD-11F that lost its No.1 engine/pylon on takeoff from Louisville, killing 3 crew and 11 on the ground. Trade press reporting on the hearing describes fatigue cracking in an aft pylon spherical bearing (P/N S00399-1), prior bearing-failure incidents referenced in a 2011 Boeing service letter (MD-11-SL-54-104-A), and Boeing testimony that the bearing should have been classified as a Principal Structural Element at original certification. The FAA's emergency AD grounded MD-11/DC-10/MD-10 variants in November 2025; that grounding was eased in May 2026 per trade press.

Technical detail: NTSB docket DCA26MA024 — Investigative Hearing [VERIFIED — primary source] [source]; AD FAA emergency AD (MD-11/DC-10/MD-10 grounding, Nov 2025) [UNVERIFIED — reference exists, text not accessed]; Reported (trade press characterization): fatigue cracking in the bearing race propagated until overstress fracture of both lugs, shifting load and allowing engine/pylon separation.; Reported (trade press characterization of hearing testimony): Boeing staff acknowledged the failed aft pylon spherical bearing should have been classified as a Principal Structural Element during original MD-11 certification..

“The three crewmembers aboard the airplane were killed. Eleven people on the ground were killed and another 23 injured.” — Investigative Hearing: UPS Flight 2976 Crash During Takeoff, DCA26MA024, 2026-05-19 [source]

Read-across to fleet: Classic major industry event — pylon/engine-mount structural integrity and the OEM-service-letter-vs-mandatory-AD gap is a read-across theme for all CX fleet types, even though CX operates no MD-11/DC-10.
