**A321neo (XWB sub-variants) — FAA AD 2026-12-11 effective August 3, 2026: cold working process deviation at center fuselage frame foot joint connections, all CX fleet variants explicitly named** — A321neo, 2026-06-26.

*What happened:* The FAA adopted AD 2026-12-11 (Amendment 39-23381, effective August 3, 2026) for A321-251NX, -252NX, -253NX, -271NX, and -272NX — all Cathay Pacific A321neo XWB sub-variants. A deviation to the cold working assembly-line manufacturing process was found at fastener holes in the center fuselage frame foot joint connections at FR37–FR41 between STR21–STR23. Repetitive rototest or high-frequency eddy current inspections are required; corrective actions are mandatory where discrepancies are found.

*Technical detail:* FAA AD 2026-12-11 (Amendment 39-23381) / FR doc 2026-13062 / docket FAA-2026-3485, Airbus A321-251NX, -252NX, -253NX, -271NX, -272NX; effective August 3, 2026 [VERIFIED — primary source] ([source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-29/html/2026-13062.htm)). Repetitive rototest or HFEC inspection of fastener holes at FR37–FR41/STR21–STR23 required to verify nominal design condition; corrective actions per Airbus, FAA, or EASA approval. Root cause: assembly-line cold working process deviation at center fuselage frame foot joint connections; could adversely affect fatigue life of affected fastener holes.

*Verbatim:* "a deviation to the manufacturing process" — Airworthiness Directives; Airbus SAS Airplanes, FAA AD 2026-12-11, 2026-06-29, [link](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-29/html/2026-13062.htm).

**A350-900/-1000 — FAA AD 2026-12-05 effective July 20, 2026: primary flight control actuators exposed to mechanical overloads during production acceptance test; all affected units must be replaced** — A350-900, A350-1000, 2026-06-15. *Carried-over developing event.*

*What happened:* The FAA adopted AD 2026-12-05 (effective July 20, 2026) for all Airbus A350-941 and A350-1041 airplanes — both types in the Cathay Pacific fleet. Certain primary flight control actuators — servo controls, electrical backup hydraulic actuators (EBHAs), and electro-hydrostatic actuators (EHAs) — were exposed to mechanical overloads during the production acceptance test procedure, reducing their fatigue life and risking control surface or hydraulic system failure. All affected units must be replaced; re-installation is prohibited. FAA estimates 38 U.S.-registered aircraft are affected.

*Technical detail:* FAA AD 2026-12-05 / FR doc 2026-11975 / docket FAA-2026-3473, all Airbus A350-941 and A350-1041 airplanes; effective July 20, 2026 [VERIFIED — primary source] ([source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-15/html/2026-11975.htm)). Replace all affected primary flight control actuators with serviceable parts; prohibit re-installation; comply with EASA AD 2025-0152 (July 18, 2025). Root cause: mechanical overloads applied during production acceptance test; reduced fatigue life risking control surface failure or hydraulic system loss.

*Verbatim:* "have been exposed to mechanical overloads during the acceptance test procedure" — Airworthiness Directives; Airbus SAS Airplanes, FAA AD 2026-12-05, 2026-06-15, [link](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-15/html/2026-11975.htm).

**A350-900 — FAA AD 2026-12-04 effective July 14, 2026: improper fastener retorque at CWB/belly fairing junctions; EN6115 code B rotation risk; fuel leak and ignition hazard; A350-1041 NOT in scope** — A350-900, 2026-06-09. *Carried-over developing event.*

*What happened:* The FAA adopted AD 2026-12-04 (effective July 14, 2026) for all Airbus A350-941 airplanes. A350-1041 is confirmed NOT in scope. A manufacturing investigation found improper application of the fastener retorque process at the center wing box and belly fairing junctions; EN6115 code B fasteners susceptible to rotation when insufficiently torqued could create an ignition risk in a fuel-leak scenario. The AD requires replacement of affected fasteners and application of additional head nut cap protection per Airbus SB A350-57-P093 (dated June 17, 2025). Triggered by EASA AD 2025-0209 (September 24, 2025).

*Technical detail:* FAA AD 2026-12-04 / FR doc 2026-11511 / docket FAA-2026-3475, all Airbus A350-941 airplanes (A350-1041 NOT in scope — confirmed from primary source); effective July 14, 2026 [VERIFIED — primary source] ([source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-09/html/2026-11511.htm)). Replace affected fasteners on LH and RH CWB/belly fairing junctions; apply additional head nut cap protection per Airbus SB A350-57-P093 (dated June 17, 2025). Root cause: improper fastener retorque at CWB/belly fairing junctions; EN6115 code B fasteners susceptible to rotation causing insufficient clamping and potential fuel-leak ignition risk.

*Verbatim:* "improper application of the fastener retorque process at the center wing box (CWB) and belly fairing (BF) junctions could lead to insufficient clamping" — Airworthiness Directives; Airbus SAS Airplanes, FAA AD 2026-12-04, 2026-06-09, [link](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-09/html/2026-11511.htm).
