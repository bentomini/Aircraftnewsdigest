## Directly Fleet-Relevant

**EASA emergency AD 2026-0172-E: Trent 700 oil-pump shear-neck defect, mandatory de-pairing on A330** — A330-300, 2026-09-04.
*What happened:* EASA issued emergency AD 2026-0172-E (issued 4 Sept 2026, effective 8 Sept 2026) for Rolls-Royce Trent 700 engines (768-60/772-60/772B-60/772C-60 variants, powering the A330-200/-300, A330 MRTT and Beluga XL), mandating de-pairing of affected oil pumps — no more than one non-conforming pump per twin-engine aircraft — following reports of low oil pressure linked to a potential failure mode at the oil-pump input coupling shear neck. The FAA reportedly adopted a companion emergency AD (2026-18-51) on 5 Sept 2026; that document could not be located on federalregister.gov or drs.faa.gov and remains unverified.
*Technical detail:* EAD 2026-0172-E, effectivity Rolls-Royce RB211 Trent 768-60/772-60/772B-60/772C-60 engines (A330-200/-300, A330 MRTT, Beluga XL) [VERIFIED — primary source] ([source](https://ad.easa.europa.eu/ad/2026-0172-E)); AD 2026-18-51 [UNVERIFIED — reference exists, text not accessed] ([reporting](https://www.aviacionline.com/english/manufacturers-mro/faa-adopts-two-easa-emergency-ads-for-rolls-royce-trent-700-and-trent-800-engines_a6a9c7c10ff4353f8819744bb)). EASA AD requires de-pairing (no more than one affected oil pump per twin-engine aircraft); no fleet grounding ordered; special flight permits prohibited, per trade press describing the EASA action. Root cause: non-conforming oil pumps with low accumulated time; potential failure mode at the oil-pump input coupling shear neck; the detailed unsafe-condition narrative sits in the AD's PDF attachment, which could not be reliably extracted, so it is not asserted as fetched fact.

**FAA AD 2026-17-09: missing fastener chamfers on A350-1000 engine pylons** — A350-1000, 2026-09-03.
*What happened:* FAA published AD 2026-17-09 (docket FAA-2026-8787, FR doc 2026-18055) for Airbus A350-1041 airplanes on 3 Sept 2026, addressing missing chamfers under the heads of critical fasteners on the primary structure of both engine pylons. The AD requires inspection of affected fastener areas and corrective action if discrepancies are found, incorporating EASA AD 2025-0106 by reference. Effective 18 Sept 2026; the FAA states there are currently no US-registered aircraft in the affected population.
*Technical detail:* AD 2026-17-09, effectivity Airbus A350-1041 airplanes, as identified in EASA AD 2025-0106 (exact MSN list not given in the FAA text itself) [VERIFIED — primary source] ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/html/2026/09/03/2026-18055.html)). FAA: uncorrected condition could reduce fatigue life and adversely affect pylon structural integrity; zero US-registered aircraft currently in the affected population. Root cause: missing chamfers under fastener heads on both engine-pylon primary structures, per the FAA AD text.
*Verbatim:* "Missing chamfers under the heads of critical fasteners on the primary structure of both pylons." — Airworthiness Directives; Airbus SAS Airplanes (AD 2026-17-09), 2026-17-09, 2026-09-03, [link](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/html/2026/09/03/2026-18055.html).

**FAA AD 2026-18-03: A320-family main landing gear support rib 5 tool-mark damage, includes A321neo (NX)** — A321neo, 2026-09-03.
*What happened:* FAA AD 2026-18-03 (docket FAA-2026-8793, FR doc 2026-18052), effective 18 Sept 2026, addresses tool-mark damage in the bores of forward and aft lugs on main landing gear support rib 5, caused by an improper supplier bushing-removal technique during assembly. The applicability list explicitly names A321-251NX, -253NX and -271NX — CX's A321neo sub-variants. The AD requires repetitive detailed visual inspections with a one-time special detailed inspection as terminating action; 29 US-registered airplanes are affected.
*Technical detail:* AD 2026-18-03, effectivity A318-112; A319-115/-132/-133; A320-214/-216/-232/-233/-251N/-271N; A321-211/-213/-231/-271N/-251NX/-253NX/-271NX [VERIFIED — primary source] ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/html/2026/09/03/2026-18052.html)). FAA: fatigue damage to rib 5 could lead to structural failure/collapse of the main landing gear; 29 US-registered aircraft affected. Root cause: improper bushing-removal technique used by a supplier during assembly, causing tool-mark damage to lug bores, per the FAA AD text.
*Verbatim:* "Tool mark damage in the bore of the forward and aft lugs." — Airworthiness Directives; Airbus SAS Airplanes (AD 2026-18-03), 2026-18-03, 2026-09-03, [link](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/html/2026/09/03/2026-18052.html).
