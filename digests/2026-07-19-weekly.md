**FAA supersedes LEAP-1A HPT blade AD to mandate South Asia borescope inspection regime — two in-flight shutdowns cited** — A321neo, LEAP-1A, 2026-07-10.

*What happened:* FAA issued AD superseding 2025-21-03 for CFM LEAP-1A engines, prompted by two in-flight shutdowns caused by HPT rotor stage 1 blade cracking driven by accelerated dust-induced deterioration in the South Asia region. Operators must perform an initial BSI within 100 flight cycles after accumulating 1,100 South Asia takeoffs on the affected blade, with repeat inspections based on findings. South Asia is defined as Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, and Sri Lanka.

*Technical detail:* AD FR-2026-13979, effective 2026-07-27; effectivity: CFM LEAP-1A23, LEAP-1A24, LEAP-1A24E1, LEAP-1A26, LEAP-1A26CJ, LEAP-1A26E1, LEAP-1A29, LEAP-1A29CJ, LEAP-1A30, LEAP-1A32, LEAP-1A33, LEAP-1A33B2, LEAP-1A35A engines. Root cause: dust accumulation during South Asia operations causes accelerated HPT stage 1 blade deterioration and cracking. FAA determination: engines operating in the South Asia region are susceptible to accelerated HPT rotor stage 1 blade deterioration and airfoil distress, leading to cracking and in-flight shutdown risk. ## Major Industry Events

**Iberia A321XLR and Air Europa 787-9 in near head-on collision over Atlantic (July 10) — TCAS averts collision after ATC assigns same altitude opposing airways** — A321neo, 787, 2026-07-10.

*What happened:* An Iberia Airbus A321XLR (EC-OLE, IB-140, Recife to Madrid) and an Air Europa Boeing 787-9 (EC-ODH, UX-57, Madrid to São Paulo) came within fewer than 2 nautical miles at the same altitude (FL360) over the Atlantic near the West Saharan coast at approximately 01:23 UTC on July 10, 2026. Both aircraft were assigned airway N857 in opposite directions at FL360 by ATC. Simultaneous TCAS resolution advisories commanded the 787 to climb and the A321XLR to descend. Both aircraft continued safely to their destinations with no damage or injuries. Primary cause appears to be an ATC separation failure.

*Technical detail:* TCAS RAs performed as designed on both aircraft; no aircraft system faults reported. [REPORTED — trade press, unconfirmed]

*Read-across to Cathay Pacific fleet:* ATC-caused near-collision; TCAS functioned correctly on both aircraft. No aircraft system fault.

**K2 Airways Boeing 737-400F hull loss into Arabian Sea (July 7) — 5 crew presumed dead, navigation malfunction reported prior to rapid descent** — 737-400, 2026-07-07.

*What happened:* K2 Airways cargo flight KTA-1732, Boeing 737-4M0(BDSF) AP-BOI, disappeared over the Arabian Sea on July 7, 2026 while operating Sharjah to Karachi. The crew reported a navigation system malfunction approximately 155 nautical miles from Karachi; ADS-B data showed a subsequent rapid descent to 1,100 feet at -22,400 fpm before contact was lost. Wreckage was found 53 nautical miles from Ormara, Pakistan. All five crew members are presumed dead. Pakistan's Aircraft Accident Investigation Board has opened a formal investigation.

*Read-across to Cathay Pacific fleet:* Hull loss of a commercial cargo aircraft with 5 fatalities; navigation system malfunction followed by catastrophic descent is a major industry safety event under active investigation.
